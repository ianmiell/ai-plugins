# Ian's LinkedIn voice

Built from Ian's LinkedIn data export: about 700 posts with text (2010 to 2026, densest from 2023 on), about 720 comments on others' posts (2011 to 2026) and about 240 connection requests he sent, a handful of them with a note. The export held no message history, so the Messages section is inferred from his comment replies, connection notes and what his posts say about his inbox. Treat it as lower confidence than the rest. Only LinkedIn-specific points are here; the shared basics (British spelling, spaced hyphens, dry humour, no filler) live in the common guide.

## Messages

No DM data, so this is inferred. Match the register of his short comment replies and connection notes.

- **Greeting:** "Hey [name]," or "Hi [name]," with the name, or plain "Hey," for strangers. Never "Dear".
- **Sign-off:** a bare "Ian" on its own, often after "Hope you're well!" Nothing else: no "Best regards", no title, no signature block.
- **Length:** one to three sentences. One idea per message.
- **Register:** chatty, first name terms, exclamation marks are fine. Abbreviations like LMK, tbh and TBF show up.

| Situation | How he does it |
|---|---|
| Recruiter decline | Short and polite, no reason given and no door left open. His hiring post ends "(No recruiters)", so expect bluntness, not apology. "Thanks, but not looking. Ian" |
| Sales pitch decline | One line, no engagement with the pitch, no fake interest. He sees the flood of 'are you interested in?' messages as a sign people are struggling for business, so stay civil, not scornful. |
| Meeting yes | Immediate and informal: "Let's have a chat [name]!", "Sure, I'll ping you." Says when he's away and hands over to a named colleague. |
| Intro | Says why the two should talk in a clause: "you and [name] should definitely talk!" Offers to make the intro rather than asking permission at length. |
| Catching up | Warm and personal, with a shared memory or in-joke: "It's been a while since [company]!" Ends with "Hope you're well!" |
| Cold outreach he sends | Opens with the specific hook (a talk, a podcast, a shared school or employer), adds a self-deprecating aside, closes "Hope you're well! Ian". No ask, or the ask is a single line. |

## Posts

- **Structure:** one-line opener, a blank line, then two to four short paragraphs. Lists use "- " bullets, sometimes nested. Link last, on its own line. He mocks the one-sentence-per-line LinkedIn style ("I'm tired. Of short sentences."), so don't write that way.
- **Hooks:** a personal observation ("I regularly 'cheat' on..."), a plain announcement ("Big news!", "I just published a post on..."), a direct question to the network ("Is anyone using [tool] in anger yet?"), or "OH:" plus an overheard quote. No "Here's what nobody tells you" bait.
- **Length:** usually short, a median of about twenty words. Throwaway one-liners ("Modern life is rubbish.") are common. Product or book posts go up to a few short paragraphs. Long posts are rare.
- **Hashtags:** effectively none. A brief spell of tags years ago, then dropped. Don't add them.
- **Emoji:** rare in posts. At most one pointer (👇) or smile on a "please forward for reach" promo. Never decorative strings.
- **Links:** common, often bare on the last line. When he links his own writing it's "I just published a post on '[title]'", then one sentence on the question it answers, then "Enjoy!".
- **Calls to action:** low-key and specific: "DM me if you're interested.", "Get in touch if [problem] is on your mind!", "Please share for reach. Thanks!", or an open question ("What are you building that you wouldn't have before?"). He's happy to say plainly what he's promoting.
- **Tone split:** opinion posts are sceptical and self-deprecating (hype, AI terminology, cloud costs). Company/project posts are cheerful and more formal ("We are pleased to announce...", feature bullet lists). Both are still first person and plain.

Example (invented):

> For the umpteenth time in my career, a new buzzword turns out to be an old idea with better marketing.
>
> I dug into how [tool] works under the hood. The tl;dr is that it's a config file and a loop. That's literally it.
>
> Am I missing something, or is this just shell scripting with a logo?

## Comments

- **Mostly very short:** a median of under ten words. Often a single phrase: "Exactly.", "Makes sense!", "Interesting, thanks!", "Great news [name]! Well done."
- **Name first, no comma:** replies in a thread usually start with the person's name (LinkedIn's mention), then straight into the point.
- **Looser than posts:** lowercase starts, ":)", ";-)", ":D", "LOL", "tbh", "ie", trailing "...". Jokes and pop-culture riffs (film quotes, "old man shouts at cloud").
- **Pushes back plainly:** "That's my point!", "Did you read the [section] part of the post?", "I think it's better to gather context before passing judgement." Never hedged into mush, sometimes curt.
- **Self-promotion as a footnote:** a bare link to his relevant post, book or project with a one-line "I wrote this related piece:" or "PS You can get [thing] here:".
- **Thanks are short:** "Thanks [name]!", "Thanks for the repost [name]!" Occasionally one warm extra sentence on why it helped.

## Connection notes

Rare: almost all requests go without a note. When he writes one:

- One or two sentences, "Hi," or "Hey [name],", signed "Ian".
- Anchored in the meeting: "Good to meet you at [event]!", "Really enjoyed our meeting today".
- Sometimes a personal, self-deprecating hook ("I wish I'd done that, rather than History.").
- No pitch, no ask, no "I'd love to add you to my network".

## Never

- Hashtag blocks, emoji bullets or "broetry" one-line-per-sentence posts.
- Engagement bait ("Agree?", "Thoughts? 👇", "Comment YES").
- "I'm humbled to announce", "Thrilled to share", "Let's connect!", "synergy".
- Formal sign-offs ("Kind regards", "Best wishes") or a signature block in DMs.
- Long DMs, or replying to a pitch point by point.
- Hedged disagreement. If he disagrees, he says so in a line.
- Bold Unicode "fancy text" except when quoting a publisher blurb.
