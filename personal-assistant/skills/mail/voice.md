# Ian's email voice

Read this before writing any email draft. It was built in October 2026 from
Ian's own sent mail: about 186 work messages (Feb–Oct 2026) and about 280
personal ones (Oct 2024–Oct 2026). Use the section for the mailbox the draft
goes from. The two overlap a lot. The differences are mostly the signature,
how formal he is with clients, and how relaxed he is with old colleagues.

A draft should read as if Ian typed it himself in under a minute. When in
doubt, make it **shorter**.

## Common to both

- **Get straight to the point.** No preamble and no recap of the other
  person's email. Most of his emails are 1–3 sentences, and many are one line
  ("Have booked.", "Done, thanks.", "Yup, that works.").
- **Paragraphs are short:** 1–3 sentences each, with a blank line between them.
  Greeting and sign-off go on their own lines. He only writes more than about
  150 words when the content needs it (feedback, a proposal, a complaint, a
  meeting recap). Then the order is: context, then `- ` bullets or `1)`
  points, then the ask.
- **Use British spelling and idiom:** organisation, licence (the noun),
  favour, "in the diary", "give me a shout", "pop down", "sort out", "my 2p".
- **Use contractions throughout:** I'll, we're, don't, it's, let's. He often
  drops the subject on quick notes: "Have fixed.", "Am asking around now.",
  "Put something in for 11-12."
- **Use a spaced hyphen " - " as the dash. Never an em dash (—).** This is
  the most obvious giveaway of a machine-written draft.
- **Parentheses** are for asides: "(travel permitting!)", "(ie value stream
  mapping)". Write "ie" and "eg" with no full stops. Use single quotes for
  scare quotes ('war story', 'quick swap'), slashes for alternatives
  ("and/or", "is/is not"), and plain abbreviations: BTW, FYI, wrt, NB, TBD,
  F2F, "CC'ing".
- **Exclamation marks** are fine, at most one per email, for warmth or
  good news ("That's great news, thanks!"). Never use emoji. A ":)" or ";-)"
  is OK in a friendly email, at most once.
- **Humour is dry and understated.** It's often self-deprecating or a wry
  aside. Never force it.
- **Questions are short and direct:** "How quick?", "Can you confirm?",
  "Does Thursday afternoon work for you at all?"
- **End with a concrete next step or a "Let me know..." line**, e.g. "Let
  me know what you think.", "Let me know when works for you.", "Let me know if
  you need more info."
- **Sign with just "Ian".** Leave out "Best wishes", "Warm regards", "Many
  thanks" and "Best regards", except for strangers or very formal mail.
- **Don't copy his typos or lower-case one-liners.** Those are phone habits,
  and a draft shouldn't invent them.

### Phrases he actually uses

- "As discussed, here is [X]." / "As promised, here is [X]."
- "Here's my calendly if it helps find a time: [link]" (his Calendly link
  goes on the next line)
- "Let me know when's a good time to [catch up / demo X]."
- "Just a nudge on this one, in case it got lost." / "Gentle reminder about
  this."
- "Is there any update on [X]? I haven't heard anything as yet."
- "All good, just wanted to make sure it didn't get lost."
- "I'll await the invite."
- "Looping in [name] here." / "I'm CC'ing [name], who [role]."
- "[A], meet [B]. [B], meet [A]."
- "Happy to discuss further if you'd like."
- "A quick heads-up on this one - ..."
- "For clarity: ..."
- "Thanks, but we have no need for this." / "I'll pass, thanks."
- "...as I wouldn't want to waste your time."
- "Sadly I can't make [date]..."
- "Sorry for not responding earlier - [one-clause reason]."
- "Thanks for chasing."
- "Please confirm receipt."
- "That's great news, thanks! Looking forward to it."
- "Not sure if this helps, but it's my experience :)"

### Never

- "I hope this email finds you well", "Please do not hesitate to contact
  me", "at your earliest convenience", "per my last email", "circle back",
  "touch base", "reach out", "synergy"
- Stacked pleasantries, gushing thanks, long or repeated apologies,
  overselling, or flowery adjectives
- Headings, heavy bold, emoji, "x"s or kisses (not even to family), "Dear
  all", "Hello", "Good morning"
- Restating what the other person said before answering

### By situation

| Situation | How he does it |
|---|---|
| Yes / accept | One line, sometimes with one condition. "Yes, but I have a hard stop at 1.30." / "Let's go for Tuesday at 3pm." |
| Decline | Thanks, then one short reason. No apology. Often keeps the door open: "Thanks, but I'm not looking at the moment. Happy to keep in touch though." |
| Decline scope or price (work) | "Thanks for your email. Unfortunately, on reflection, we can't [X]." Then say what they *could* do, then "Happy to discuss further." Firm, no hedging. |
| Chase | One or two lines, polite, no guilt-tripping, occasionally a light joke ("Bump on this one, as it's a Friday!"). |
| Query a bill or quote | Calm and factual, with specific numbers, put as a question: "I make it roughly [N] - is there a reason it's higher?" |
| Formal complaint | Only when escalating: "Dear [org] team," then the facts, the remedy wanted and a deadline. |
| Scheduling | Offer two or three concrete slots, or give his Calendly link, or just say he's booked it. Offer to travel: "Happy to come to your offices." |
| Intro / delegate | "[A], meet [B]" with one line of context each. Offer to step out ("happy to stay out of it as you prefer"). When handing off, CC the person and name their role. |
| Delay apology | One honest clause, then a realistic new date: "Sorry, I've been snowed under - realistically it'll be the end of the month. Let me know if that causes problems." |
| Thanks | Brief. "Thanks [name]." / "Great, thanks for the update!" / "Really appreciate the fast turnaround on this." |
| Meeting recap (work) | "To summarise the key points:", then bullets, then actions agreed with the owner in **bold**, then "Please let me know if I've missed anything out here." |

## Work mailbox (ian.miell@container-solutions.com)

He writes as CTO of Container Solutions: plain, direct and dry, somewhere
between friendly and businesslike, with an engineer's precision.

**Greeting**, by audience:
- **Reply mid-thread or to a colleague:** no greeting. Start with the content.
  This is the default, about half of his emails.
- **First message to a client, prospect or vendor, or a formal follow-up:**
  "Hi [name]," on its own line.
- **Warm contacts, partners and founders he's met, and cold inbound he's
  turning down:** "Hey [name],". On a one-liner it can be inline: "Hey [name] -
  am away Monday..."
- **A crisp business point, often to several people:** "[name]," or
  "[name]/[name],".
- **Replying to an introduction:** "Thanks [introducer]." then, on the next
  line, "Hi [new person],".

**Sign-off:**
- **Colleagues and quick replies:** "Ian", or nothing at all on a one-word
  answer.
- **Most other mail:** "Thanks," / blank line / "Ian".
- **Clients, prospects, senior or outside people:** "Kind regards," / blank
  line / "Ian".
- **Friendly peers and founders:** sometimes "Cheers, Ian".
- **Signature block:** his desktop mail ends with a CTO signature, but never
  type one into a draft. For a new email to someone outside the company,
  mention that Ian may want to add it before sending.

**Tone by audience:**
- **Colleagues:** terse and opinionated, with a clear judgement ("I wouldn't
  recommend that, as it's too risky.") and dry humour. Usually no greeting
  and no sign-off.
- **Clients and prospects:** fuller sentences and a little context ("First,
  let me set a little context."), specific next steps and an offer to visit.
  Honest about limits ("we don't want to pretend we have an off-the-shelf
  solution"). Rarely uses exclamation marks.
- **Cold inbound, recruiters and vendors:** very short, sceptical,
  sometimes wry. Polite, but closes the door.
- **Suppliers and admin:** functional: "Hi [name], After consideration, we
  will take [X]. Please forward the invoice. Thanks, Ian"

Example:

> Hi Sarah,
>
> Thanks for your time today - good to meet you!
>
> As discussed, I attach the one-pager. Let me know when's a good time for a
> demo, as that's probably the quickest way to get into the detail. Here's my
> calendly if it helps:
> [link]
>
> Kind regards,
>
> Ian

> Yup, fine by me. I'd push back on the start date though - we won't have
> anyone free until the 12th.
> Ian

## Personal mailbox (ian.miell@gmail.com)

Most of his personal email is life admin and professional side work:
publishers and course organisers, solicitors, agents, tradespeople, his
accountant, recruiters and old colleagues. Friends and family mostly reach
him elsewhere. The register is polite, plain and businesslike, but relaxed.
**There is no signature block**, ever.

**Greeting:**
- **Default:** "Hi [name]," on its own line.
- **People he knows and likes** (editors, old colleagues): "Hey [name],".
- **Quick replies in an ongoing thread:** no greeting.
- **Being brisk or firm** (e.g. querying a quote): "[name],".
- **A business he hasn't contacted before:** "Hi," or "Dear [org] team,"
  only for a formal complaint.

**Sign-off:**
- **Standard:** "Thanks," / blank line / "Ian".
- **After a short substantive note:** just "Ian".
- **One-liners:** inline, e.g. "Done, thanks. Ian".
- **First contact with solicitors, businesses or publishers:** "Kind
  regards,".
- **Rarely:** "Speak soon!", "Cheers,", "All the best,".
- **Ultra-short admin replies** ("Paid", "Done."): unsigned.

**Tone by audience:**
- **Tradespeople and services:** a factual brief (what, where, when), then
  "Please let me know if you're interested in the job." Mild warmth ("Hope
  all is well.").
- **Solicitors, banks and officials:** facts on separate lines or numbered,
  then "Please confirm", then "Kind regards,". Precise and cooperative.
- **Publishers and course contacts:** friendly, with an exclamation mark on
  good news. Candid about delays. Opinionated but hedged ("Not sure how
  important that is, but...").
- **Old colleagues and peers:** his most relaxed voice: "Hey", a joke or
  ";-)", self-deprecation, "my 2p", and often ending "How's things with you?"
- **Recruiters and strangers:** one or two lines, polite, signed just "Ian".
- **Family:** bare logistics, with no greeting or sign-off.

Example:

> Hey [name],
>
> Great to hear from you! Things are busy here - lots more AI work in the day
> job, which has been fun (and occasionally terrifying ;-)).
>
> Would be good to catch up properly. Here's my calendly if it helps:
> [link]
>
> How's things with you?
>
> Ian

> Hi [name],
>
> Thanks for this. Can you break the quote down a bit more? I make the
> materials at roughly [N] - is there a reason it's higher?
>
> Thanks,
>
> Ian
